# Split Testing

Obtain the least-cost sufficient comparative evidence for the live action, or establish honestly why no valid comparison is available, without making the user invent an evaluation method. Sufficiency is strength for the action and its consequences, not test volume, precision, polish, or resemblance to a familiar evaluation.

Establish what the action actually depends on: rightful authority, intended population and horizon, consequences and vetoes, alternatives, and the system policy under which they would be used. Infer ordinary observations, conditions, instruments, variation, and stopping. Ask only when an unresolved value, authority, permission, safety, external effect, or consequential budget could reverse or prohibit the action. If clarification would merely manufacture one winner from a supported tradeoff, preserve the conditional result.

Reuse evidence that already resolves the live distinction. Otherwise arrange only observations capable of changing the action, revealing a veto or genuine tradeoff, invalidating an inference, or identifying an actionable cause. Bind retries, replacement, assistance, resources, selection, and deployment behavior into the tested condition. Protect inclusion, adaptation, stopping, and review-completion choices from result-sensitive revision. Keep representative evidence separate from diagnostic challenges, and start a new prospective diagnostic observation when candidate outputs inform it.

Treat every instruction surface as part of exposure. Give each function complete legitimate authority, sources, inputs, tools, boundaries, and the real consumer interface while withholding comparative purpose, expected winners, sibling outputs, hidden resolution logic, and preferred methods that intended use would not supply. Fresh context is useful only for the influences it separates; it must not become impoverished context.

Resolve each consequential property through the nearest legitimate evidence and keep every instrument subordinate to rightful authority. Validate a consequential instrument when a plausible defect could reverse the action, hide a veto, or make the property unobservable. After semantic judgment, verify each decisive factual premise against stronger retained evidence before using it. Keep behavior separate from causal explanation and report non-identifiability when accessible evidence cannot distinguish task, candidate, environment, or instrument explanations.

Preserve every distinction in outcomes, conditions, populations, horizons, constraints, tails, interactions, and uncertainty whose collapse could change the action or hide a non-compensable failure. Do not require dimensions, multiple scores, an aggregate, a frontier, or a result-class label when a simpler expression is adequate. Apply no arbitrary cap to alternatives, agents, reviewers, observations, or attempts; queue work under real limits and stop when further evidence lacks expected decision value.

Open only the reference whose additional detail can change the current decision:

- [Arranging evidence](references/arranging-evidence.md) when existing evidence is not fit or observation design remains consequential.
- [Information and execution](references/information-and-execution.md) when agents, people, prompts, tools, environments, files, sessions, or shared state will produce evidence.
- [Validity and attribution](references/validity-and-attribution.md) when an instrument, reviewer, uncertainty, causal claim, or failure attribution controls the conclusion.
- [Presenting evidence](references/presenting-evidence.md) only after evidence exists and presentation can materially improve the real consumer's decision or use.

Return the least-complex adequate expression of what the evidence supports, its consequential limits, and what would reopen it. A conversational answer is sufficient for a simple live choice. Verify a durable boundary only when files, links, packages, executables, or another persistent consumer interface are actually delivered.
