# Obtaining Evidence

Use fit existing evidence before arranging new observations. Evidence is fit only when its authority, population, horizon, tested system policy, conditions, and resolution boundary bear on the live decision. Familiarity, prestige, scale, or prior reuse does not establish fit.

When new evidence is needed, arrange the smallest set of observations capable of changing the action, revealing a veto or genuine tradeoff, invalidating the inference, or identifying an actionable cause. The result may be ephemeral for one decision or retained for reruns; reuse and packaging are consequences of the decision, not presumed forms of comparison.

Derive observations from intended use, governing authority, the live alternatives, and plausible decision-reversing failure generators. Prefer a minimally redundant set that can expose every remaining material risk. This is a reasoning aim, not an algorithm, worksheet, or required artifact. Do not add a case, distinction, score, reviewer, document, or stage merely to make the work look comprehensive.

Keep evidence intended to support ordinary-use, population, frequency, prevalence, or expected-consequence claims separate from evidence selected to expose a boundary, veto, shortcut, causal possibility, rare severe failure, or validity defect. A challenge informed by candidate behavior begins a new prospective diagnostic round. It cannot be relabeled as representative evidence, even when the challenge is realistic.

Vary conditions only when the change could alter the operative behavior, expose an interaction or veto, reverse the action, or test whether the instrument responds to the target rather than a nuisance. Visible difficulty labels, prompt size, arbitrary case totals, and apparent sophistication do not establish useful variation.

Several consequential aspects may need independent observation even when one outcome is primary. Do not force multiple scores or a dimension inventory. Instead, refuse to collapse any distinction in outcomes, conditions, populations, horizons, constraints, or uncertainty when the collapse could reverse the action, conceal a veto or genuine non-dominance, or invalidate the inference. Discard distinctions that cannot matter.

Choose repetition to re-expose the actual source of variation. When variation matters, freeze an initial evidence budget, replacement policy, and stopping rule before results can influence them; adapt only in a new prospective round. Deterministic evidence may require no repetition. Replaying an unchanged deterministic step does not create independent observations of a variable agent, person, or environment. Stop arranging observations when a decisive requirement is settled, the supported action is stable across remaining plausible uncertainty, no authorized identifying observation exists, or the expected decision value of more evidence is below its cost or risk.
