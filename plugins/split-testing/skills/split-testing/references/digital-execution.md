# Digital Execution

Use digital execution only when it can observe a live decision property. Agents, commands, sandboxes, terminals, files, and logs are means of exposure and capture; their presence does not make an observation independent, representative, or valid.

Reconstruct the executor's actual deployed input stack and authority rather than passing the controller's full context. Supply the task outcome, legitimate facts, inputs, constraints, tools, and environment the intended role receives. Keep comparative rationale, suspected defects, hidden resolution logic, condition identity, sibling outputs, and evaluator-only context outside that role unless intended deployment includes them.

Use the narrowest real output interface. Do not impose a semantic template solely to simplify controller parsing or synthesis; retain native evidence that a normalizer cannot represent. If transport requires extraction or normalization, keep the raw payload and treat the transform as a separate instrument that must accept materially different valid forms and preserve hidden vetoes.

Choose boundaries that match the source of variation and contamination risk. Fresh host sessions or subagents may be useful when available. Without them, separate processes, disposable users, containers, workspaces, or terminal sessions can still support execution, while direct human exposure may be the correct unit for audience behavior. A multiplexer or process manager coordinates these units but does not isolate memory, files, credentials, environment, network state, caches, or parent instructions by itself.

When only shared state is available, identify what remains shared and either reset it, counterbalance order, redefine the unit, model observations as dependent, or narrow the claim. Never label several windows or processes independent solely because they have different process identifiers or prompts.

Keep common inputs immutable when the contrast requires them and condition state separate. Record the model, operator, host, tools, resources, assistance, retry, selection, and replacement policy needed to identify the tested condition. Capture every attempt and its mechanical terminal event before deciding publication. Infrastructure failure is an observation requiring policy application, not task failure or automatic permission to retry.

Use a disposable or faithfully simulated boundary for destructive, outward, privileged, credential-touching, or protected-state work. Do not cross an external-effect, permission, safety, or consequential-cost boundary merely because the comparison would benefit. Preserve the unobserved claim and its consequence when authorized execution is unavailable.

Do not impose a numerical ceiling on useful work. When the host limits concurrent agents or processes, queue and close units while preserving the prospective allocation, exposure, and stopping policy. Actual hardware, authority, budget, safety, and external-effect limits remain binding, and completed evidence may make additional queued work unnecessary.

Inspect current host help and capabilities rather than relying on memorized flags. Verify working directory, package path, interpreter, permissions, environment, input composition, output collection, and reset behavior at the consumer boundary. Producer-workspace success does not establish that copied, installed, converted, or delivered artifacts work.

Use the custody helper only when prospectivity, capture-before-publication, or judgment-before-reveal is a real hazard worth deterministic enforcement. Use the derived-view helper only for a persistent post-evidence package. Neither tool launches workers, creates observations, grades output, chooses criteria, aggregates results, or selects an action.
