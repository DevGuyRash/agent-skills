# Inference and Reporting

Express the supported result in the least-complex form that preserves every distinction capable of changing the action. Do not force a winner, score, vector, frontier, significance claim, or named result class. A direct choice, conditional direction, non-dominance, indistinguishability within the observed boundary, deterministic refutation, unresolved authority, causal non-identifiability, invalid comparison, or unacceptable external effect may be appropriate, but the live evidence—not this list—determines the form.

Do not aggregate outcomes merely because they can be encoded numerically. Combine them only under maker-provided weights, an adopted index owner, or a trusted consequence model. Otherwise preserve the gates, tails, interactions, populations, horizons, and tradeoffs that matter. A severe boundary failure remains visible even when ordinary performance is unchanged or a total looks favorable.

Separate what happened from why it happened. Instrument validity asks whether an observation responds to the target property and remains stable under decision-irrelevant changes. Causal identification asks whether a proposed generator produced the behavior and whether changing it would repair or generalize performance. When accessible evidence cannot distinguish plausible rival causes, report behavioral equivalence within the tested boundary and the causal limit rather than inventing a foundational explanation.

An observed failure belongs first to the tested condition, not automatically to either the task or the executor. Assign it to an underlying generator only when evidence distinguishes that generator from plausible alternatives and the attribution matters to the action; otherwise report the behavioral failure and the attribution limit.

Keep mechanical termination, infrastructure behavior, task validity, candidate behavior, instrument validity, and causal attribution distinct whenever collapsing them could change the action. An unsatisfiable or authority-incoherent task does not establish candidate failure. Failure on a valid task under the frozen tested policy is candidate evidence even if another policy could rescue it. An environment or capture failure follows the prospective retry and inclusion policy; it is neither automatic task failure nor automatic permission to rerun. When available evidence cannot distinguish task, candidate, environment, or instrument explanations, report non-identifiability.

A strong repair or generalization claim needs an intervention, ablation, counterfactual contrast, or independent held-out confirmation that could have failed under the proposed explanation. Keep unsupported diagnoses provisional. Seek the earliest actionable cause supported by evidence, not the deepest imaginable story; a one-off selection need not explain a mechanism that would not change selection, deployment, repair, or confidence.

Bound causal, population, prevalence, and deployment claims to the evidence that supports them. Diagnostic challenges can establish a boundary or possibility but not ordinary frequency. Representative observations support only the population and sampling relation they actually cover. Treat repeated outputs from shared state as dependent where that matters, and identify assistance, replacement, selection, and retry policy in the claim.

Report the action or claim supported, the decisive evidence, consequential limits, unresolved value or authority, and conditions that would reopen the conclusion. Structure is free. Use prose, data, a compact table, a persistent artifact, or another form only when it improves the consumer's decision. A conversational answer is enough for a simple live choice.

When returning `comparative-evidence-result.v1`, preserve the request `claim_id`, bind `request_digest` to the exact frozen UTF-8 request bytes, identify the tested conditions and evidence references, assess every supplied closure condition, retain scope and uncertainty without forced categories, and leave the caller's final disposition to the caller.
