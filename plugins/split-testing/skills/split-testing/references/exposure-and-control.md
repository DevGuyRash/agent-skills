# Exposure and Control

Use this route when information flow, shared state, adaptation, inclusion, or condition construction could change what the comparison means.

Treat every instruction surface as part of the tested exposure. An influence is contaminating when it can change an observation or judgment but is not part of what the decision is comparing, the intended role or deployment, governing authority, a real consumer interface, or the smallest prospectively validated control for a demonstrated integrity hazard. Freeze the controller-level decision relation and tested policies, expose each function only to the legitimate inputs, authority, boundaries, and interface it needs, retain the exact effective input stack, and capture mechanical identity outside substantive work whenever possible.

Comparability comes from a common governing relation, not necessarily byte-identical prompts, tools, resources, or output forms. Determine semantically what should be common, then retain the exact renderings needed to establish what occurred. Reconstruct each condition's effective exposure: system, developer, repository, ambient, and user instructions; attachments and source versions; tools and permissions; environment; model and reasoning setting; native scaffold; timing; retries; assistance; selection; and consumer interface. Record an unavailable or unobservable input as such rather than inventing it.

Establish which differences are intended and which make a condition compound. Every behavior-changing difference must be the intended contrast, part of a declared native alternative, or a nuisance difference that is matched, randomized, counterbalanced, measured, or retained as an inference limit. A controlled-component claim may require a common harness. An end-to-end system-choice claim may require each alternative's credible native scaffold. If both distinctions could change the action, use the least-cost contrast capable of revealing their interaction rather than automatically creating a full factorial.

Scaffolding, rubrics, examples, requested explanations, or semantic schemas are legitimate only when they are part of the tested behavior, intended deployment, a real consumer interface, a transfer of legitimate non-inferable authority or boundary, or the narrowest prospectively validated response to a demonstrated integrity hazard. Parsing convenience, visual symmetry, presumed helpfulness, and easier synthesis are insufficient. Keep model- or host-specific compensations in development evidence with the named failure, scope, prospective test, and removal condition unless native system performance is itself being compared.

Do not request process narration such as “explain how X was created” unless creating X and explaining it are independently part of the target, consumer need, causal investigation, or reproducibility contract. Otherwise seal the primary deliverable first and obtain any explanation in a separate diagnostic exposure, treating it as self-report.

Controller context governs the controller; it does not automatically become task authority for an author, executor, reviewer, audience, or deployed system. Do not leak a suspected winner, hidden criterion, comparative purpose, failure hypothesis, sibling output, preferred method, or evaluator-only fact into a function that would not receive it in intended use. Keep common material immutable where the inference depends on it and condition-local material isolated.

Blind identity when it can prevent a decision-relevant bias without removing information intrinsic to intended use. Unblinded exposure is valid when identity is part of the real experience, concealment would distort the task, or the claim does not depend on identity-neutral judgment. Blinding reduces selected information effects; it does not establish truth, independence, representative use, or valid criteria.

Fresh sessions, processes, containers, users, workspaces, hosts, or direct human exposures can create useful boundaries, but only the state and influence they actually separate count. Process coordinators such as `tmux` and `zellij` organize work; they do not themselves establish independence. When only shared state is available, redesign the independent unit, use a defensible reset or counterbalance policy, treat related observations as dependent, or narrow the claim.

Preserve exposure history and plausible familiarity with tasks, sources, instruments, or public artifacts when memorization or adaptation could invalidate the claim. Rewording known material does not automatically create fresh evidence. Use current, private, dynamically grounded, or otherwise resistant material only when the decision needs that protection.

Freeze allocation, attempt eligibility, retries, replacement, assistance, selection, inclusion, stopping, instrument adaptation, reviewer assignment, and review-completion behavior before the information they govern becomes visible. Retain failed, partial, missing, withdrawn, and excluded observations according to the committed policy. Output-informed changes begin a new prospective round and cannot rewrite earlier confirmatory evidence.

Record departures as condition changes or inference limits rather than hiding them. Rescue, asymmetric feedback, selective regeneration, retrospective exclusion, and silent reviewer replacement can each alter the tested policy even when the final artifacts appear comparable.
