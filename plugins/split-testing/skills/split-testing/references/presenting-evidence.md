# Presenting Evidence

Choose a representation only after the evidence and supported claim are established, and only when it improves the real consumer's inspection, decision, continuation, or reuse. Do not create a report, file, table, chart, dashboard, or interactive view merely because the evidence is complex or the tool is available.

Presentation is a derived, non-authoritative projection. It cannot choose what should have been observed, add or remove evidence, authorize aggregation, supply values, select a winner, turn diagnostic evidence into prevalence, or erase vetoes, missingness, uncertainty, and genuine non-dominance. Retain the native evidence and make consequential filtering, transformation, and dependencies traceable.

If presentation itself can change the consumer's action, treat it as an observation instrument and test the relevant presentation effect. A pattern first discovered after results are visible remains exploratory unless a prospective analysis already authorized that inference; reopen decision-changing discoveries prospectively.

Use the simplest fitting representation and any capable host-supported medium. Do not begin from a chart catalog, visual grammar, renderer taxonomy, favored library, dashboard convention, or standard gallery. Verify accessibility, dependencies, links, regeneration, and delivered usability only to the extent claimed by the real consumer interface.
