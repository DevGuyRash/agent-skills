# Comparative Design

Use this reference when the comparison's claim, task population, contrasts, independent units, replication, or stopping decision remains unresolved. The design models the user's decision; it is not a universal workflow.

## Claim and observation boundary

State what would change if one condition performed better and what situations the conclusion is meant to cover. Choose a comparator that answers that question: current behavior, no intervention, an earlier version, a competitor, or another justified alternative. A familiar control is not useful when it answers a different question.

Keep representative and diagnostic evidence distinct. Production-like work can support prevalence or ordinary-use claims while missing rare severe failures. A deliberately difficult boundary can prove a defect without estimating how often it occurs. Use the evidence the decision needs and keep each conclusion at its supported strength.

Place observation where the claimed property exists. If the decision concerns whether intended people or systems can use, understand, play, operate, perceive, or benefit from an alternative, exercise that role or a faithful simulation. Reviewers of an artifact can establish artifact facts and predict downstream behavior; agreement among them does not convert prediction into observed use.

Identify who owns any unresolved preference, utility, risk posture, taste, or acceptance decision. Reviewers may assess whether a candidate faithfully exposes a frontier, but their consensus does not authorize one point on that frontier for an absent maker. Apply maker-supplied or delegated values, observe the rightful decision role, or keep the result conditional.

Treat the public task, source material, authority, checker or oracle, private expectation, rubric, review interface, and report transform as one measurement system. They must agree about maker requirements, eligibility, scope, and what constitutes success. A private truth record cannot repair a contradictory public brief, and it can itself be incomplete or wrong. A derived checker that omits a consequential source branch validates only the subset it models, even when its enumeration is exhaustive.

Independent pre-exposure validation should be capable of falsifying this coherence at the final deployed boundary. Give the validator every condition-specific behavior-guiding layer, with identities removed only when that does not hide the contrast. It may reject leakage, contradictory authority, an impossible task, false checker acceptance, or an unusable review interface. It must not reject a treatment merely because the treatment is expected to fail the valid common objective; that failure may be the outcome under test. Before exposure, target evidence may require the validator to correct or extend a private key or reference validation. After exposure, such a change is a new instrument version; preserve the earlier run as measurement evidence rather than backfilling its answer key.

## Held-out work

When no task exists, create work from the real intended use. Preserve the interacting goals, constraints, inputs, variance, and plausible failure pressure that let alternatives behave differently. Difficulty alone is not discrimination. Prefer outcomes that can be inspected and whose differences would matter to the user.

Keep task-design rationale, diagnostic intent, suspected boundaries, grading logic, and condition internals outside executor-visible material. Supply every fact and constraint a real user would have, but do not teach the contribution under test or manufacture an artificial need for it. Asking an executor to create, decide, use, play, operate, or solve is different from asking it to narrate an evaluation method.

Match exposure to the claim. A prompt that supplies an answer, criterion, or desired behavior may be valid for conformance, but it cannot establish unaided discovery, recall, surprise, or preference. Freeze held-out material without using candidate outputs or a desired winner to shape it. If outputs influence a later task or rubric, preserve that follow-up as exploratory or as a new prospective round.

When alternatives are proposed corrections, include evidence that can separate the claimed earliest cause from a patched symptom and from no effect. Recheck the exposing boundary, then use independent representative work not used to design the repair before generalizing.

## Units, variation, and topology

The experimental unit is the smallest context that can independently receive a condition. Several tasks, artifacts, measurements, or reviews from one agent, team, process, or shared session remain clustered observations. Shared files, caches, memory, rate limits, mutable services, and one parent orchestrator can also couple runs. Isolate them, block on the shared factor, or preserve the dependence in interpretation.

Use matching or blocking when the same case, starting state, host, and budget matter to every condition. Randomization protects against unknown or time-varying influences. Preserve the condition-neutral case identity needed to interpret a match through blinding; do not randomize away the correspondence.

Conditions need not be pairs. A round may compare many alternatives at once, target selected contrasts, bundle repeated runs, use teams, reserve cases for specialists, or omit mechanically decided results from semantic review. Avoid all-pairs expansion when a smaller contrast graph answers the decision, and do not infer a connected ranking from comparisons the design never made.

Breadth across cases and repeated fresh runs on one case answer different questions. Choose the evidence budget from consequence, expected variability, smallest meaningful difference, and resources. One run can prove a deterministic counterexample but not reliability. When formal confidence matters, plan from plausible effect and variance while respecting pairing and clustering.

An aggregate is a derived view, not proof that its components are interchangeable. Preserve a profile, interaction, tail, or boundary when different component patterns could change the decision; collapse it only when governing frequencies, weights, loss, or an observed invariance justify the collapse.

The same constraint applies to direct subtraction or ranking. Values drawn from different cases, populations, thresholds, deadlines, scales, or loss meanings do not become comparable because they share a unit. Use a common consequence boundary or an adopted normalization; otherwise keep the observations distinct and do not present their numerical difference as an option benefit.

The kernel's three-repetition default protects only against mistaking one completion for a condition. It is not statistical proof. Place those repetitions at the unit that can actually vary. Three fresh agent completions can expose agent variability; three identical calls to a source-faithful deterministic program within one completion do not become three agent trials or strengthen a fact the first call already established. Repeat a nominally deterministic mechanism only when nondeterminism, state, flakiness, or environment dependence is itself plausible and consequential. Preserve every failure and incomplete result. Best-of-N selection is a different system and is valid only when that selection mechanism is part of the declared condition.

Freeze stopping and replacement rules before observing outcomes. Replace only a compromised run under a validity rule applied symmetrically; an unfavorable result is not invalid. If evidence is inspected while the budget grows, keep the round exploratory or use an analysis valid under that stopping rule. Multiple contrasts and repeated looks require honest uncertainty rather than the most favorable isolated significance result.

## Models, hosts, and later rounds

Match model, resolved version, reasoning effort, host, tools, authority, team shape, and budget unless one is under test. A cross-host change normally bundles model, harness, ambient instruction, and tool differences; call it a compound condition unless those effects are separately isolated.

Later rounds may add alternatives or target uncertainty revealed earlier. Freeze each before its new outputs are examined, retain previous designs and evidence, and state why the round was added. An adaptive investigation can be useful without being misreported as one prospectively fixed proof.

## Evidence base

The local guidance is sufficient to design a comparison. These primary or fixed sources support its reopening conditions:

- [NIST experimental-design terminology](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pri/section7/pri7.htm) and [randomized block designs](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pri/section3/pri332.htm) define replication, randomization, and nuisance-factor control.
- [NIST multiple comparisons](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section4/prc47.htm) explains why repeated unadjusted contrasts can mislead.
- [Anthropic's statistical approach to model evaluations](https://www.anthropic.com/research/statistical-approach-to-model-evals) demonstrates material uncertainty error from clustered questions.
- [On Randomness in Agentic Evals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07150v3) relates repetition needs to run variance, effect size, and power.
- [PACE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08106v1) reports harmful false acceptance under greedy repeated optimization on noisy held-out scores.
- [OpenAI's coding-evaluation audit](https://openai.com/index/separating-signal-from-noise-coding-evaluations/) documents consequential failures from broken tasks, misleading prompts, and weak checks.
