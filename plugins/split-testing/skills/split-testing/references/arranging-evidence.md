# Arranging Evidence

Use existing evidence when it observes the live property under the relevant authority, population, horizon, and system policy. Familiarity, precision, privacy, or scale does not establish fit. When no fit observation exists, arrange the smallest set capable of exposing every remaining decision-changing difference, veto, genuine tradeoff, validity failure, or actionable cause.

Derive work from intended use, governing authority, plausible boundary conditions, and the alternatives' real deployment. Do not imitate a known benchmark, convert difficulty labels or case counts into coverage, or add an observation that cannot alter the action or claim boundary. Vary a condition only when the change could alter operative behavior, reveal an interaction, expose a veto, reverse the action, or challenge the instrument.

Representative observations may support ordinary-use, prevalence, frequency, or expected-outcome claims only through their actual sampling relation. Diagnostic observations may expose a possibility, shortcut, boundary, severe rare failure, or causal hypothesis. Candidate-informed challenges begin a new prospective diagnostic observation and cannot become prevalence evidence.

Choose the unit at the smallest context that can independently receive a condition. Outputs sharing an agent, person, session, service, mutable state, or parent influence remain dependent where that matters. Match, randomize, counterbalance, reset, redefine the unit, or narrow the inference according to the actual source of variation; no topology is automatically fair.

Repetition must re-expose a real source of variation. Deterministic evidence may require no repetition. Preserve failed, partial, missing, excluded, and replaced observations under the prospective policy, and do not treat an unfavorable result as invalid. If results motivate a changed task, instrument, allocation, or stopping rule, retain the prior observation and begin a new prospective one.

Multiple designs, instruments, reviewers, or syntheses are warranted only when their disagreement could change the action or reveal instrument risk. A vote measures reviewer responses; it does not acquire decision authority. Stop when a decisive requirement is settled, the supported action is stable across remaining plausible uncertainty, no authorized identifying observation exists, or more evidence costs more than its expected decision value.
