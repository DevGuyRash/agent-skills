# Decision Warrant

Use this route when the comparison can be technically coherent yet answer the wrong question.

Identify the action or claim the evidence bears on, who rightfully controls it, the alternatives actually available, the people or systems the conclusion is meant to cover, the operating envelope and horizon, material consequences, non-compensable boundaries, and unresolved values. Treat these as relationships to preserve, not fields to display. Omit what cannot affect the action and add what the live decision requires.

Determine what “better” means under the system policy that matters to the decision. The relevant target might be the effect of one controlled change, complete alternatives under their credible native operation, strongest attainable capability under declared assistance, typical behavior in the intended workflow, or another deployment policy. These are prompts for judgment, not permitted modes. Equalizing every mechanism is not automatically fair, and allowing every native scaffold is not automatically valid; the live claim decides which differences belong in the tested condition.

Bind all behavior-changing policy to condition identity: retries, replacement, assistance, resource limits, tool access, selection among attempts, human review, fallbacks, and deployment context. Do not characterize a rescued, optimized, selected, or assisted result as ordinary unassisted single-attempt behavior. When a policy changes, the tested claim changes even if the visible output does not.

Infer the relevant policy from intended use when the evidence is clear. Ask only when materially plausible policies could reverse the action and rightful authority has neither fixed nor delegated the choice. If unresolved preference only chooses among alternatives that remain genuinely non-dominated, preserve the conditional result instead of asking merely to manufacture a winner.

Do not create an authority gap from merely imaginable consequence asymmetries. Derive the consequence relation only from supplied goals, governing constraints, and delegated choice; never treat analyst confidence as authority over taste, risk tolerance, rights, fairness, acceptable harm, or irreversible tradeoffs. For a low-cost reversible choice inside delegated authority, act provisionally and disclose a supported reversal condition. Preserve non-dominance or stop when a concrete unresolved value in the supplied context could reverse or prohibit the action.

Evidence for a class of action does not authorize you to invent decision-changing implementation details. Keep an analyst-chosen convention non-gating and conditional unless rightful authority supplied or delegated it; otherwise preserve the supported scope, ambiguity, or non-dominance.

Do not turn a bounded conclusion into an extra acceptance gate. Evidence may decisively reject one alternative without proving every broader positive claim about another. When the remaining alternatives already satisfy maker-set requirements, act on the supported asymmetry and bound or reopen the conclusion as warranted; require a stronger qualification horizon only when rightful authority or the live consequence actually makes it necessary.

Split Testing owns the generic method, not domain truth, maker values, adopted standards, or the caller's disposition. Treat supplied authority, sources, acceptance boundaries, and closure conditions as governing inputs; do not let a hidden key, evaluator consensus, or internally coherent model silently replace them.

When the input is `comparative-evidence-request.v1`, retain the exact request bytes for its digest, preserve unknown fields, respect prohibited effects, and bear the result only on the supplied claim and closure conditions. The caller decides what consequence follows after verifying claim fit.
